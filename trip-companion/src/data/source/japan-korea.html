1 hour from Tokyo. Ancient capital. Deer-free version of Nara but with the sea.
7:30 AMDepart Shinjuku → Kamakura
JR Shonan-Shinjuku Line direct to Kamakura — about 1 hour, covered by JR Pass. No transfers needed. Arrive ~8:30 AM before tour groups descend.
Train · JR Pass
8:30 AMKotoku-in — Great Buddha of Kamakura
13.35m tall bronze Buddha sitting in the open air — serene, massive, unlike anything else in Japan. Go early before tour groups. You can go inside the Buddha statue for ¥20. Photograph from the approach path looking straight at the face and from the sides for full scale. Entrance ¥300.
Photo
10:00 AMHasedera Temple
Hilltop temple with sweeping ocean views behind it. The main hall houses an 11-headed golden Kannon statue. The garden paths and viewing platform with Sagami Bay in the background is the composition shot. Entrance ¥400.
Photo
11:30 AMTsurugaoka Hachimangu Shrine
Main shrine of Kamakura, approached via a long tree-lined avenue flanked by lotus ponds. Dramatic stone staircase up to the main shrine building. Very atmospheric for photography.
Photo
1:00 PMLunch — Komachi-dori Street
The main food street leading to the shrine. Order shirasu rice — whitebait (tiny fish) on rice, a Kamakura specialty only done properly here. Also try the matcha soft serve and warabi mochi along the street.
Food
2:00 PMYuigahama or Zaimokuza Beach — Pacific Coast
15 min walk from the shrine to the Pacific Ocean. Wide beach, Enoshima Island visible in the distance, mountains behind. Walk the shoreline, get the coastal landscape shots. Completely different from the temples.
Photo
4:00 PMKamakura backstreets + small temples
Sugimoto-dera is Kamakura's oldest temple, almost always empty. Zuisen-ji temple garden is beautiful and typically uncrowded. Wander and find something quiet.

Senso-ji at Dawn + Kimonos + Akihabara + Skytree Night
5:30 AM start. Non-negotiable. One of the best days of the trip.
5:30 AM⭐ Senso-ji Temple at Dawn — Asakusa
Set multiple alarms. This is the most important early morning of the Tokyo leg. Tokyo's oldest temple at dawn — completely empty, the massive red Kaminarimon lantern lit, incense smoke drifting through the Nakamise arcade, morning mist in the courtyard. The five-story pagoda visible through the main gate. By 9 AM it's shoulder-to-shoulder tourists. At 5:30 AM it belongs to you. Bring the tripod for slow-shutter shots of the lantern and incense smoke drifting.
Photo
7:00 AMBreakfast near Senso-ji + Azuma Bridge
Small soba shop near the temple. Cross Azuma Bridge over the Sumida River — the golden flame sculpture of the Asahi Beer building is visible across the water, with Skytree behind it. Great morning composition shot with all three elements in one frame.

The covered arcade leading to Senso-ji opens around 9-10 AM — walk back after breakfast. Traditional souvenirs: ningyo-yaki (small cakes), kaminari okoshi (rice crackers), fans, wooden charms, tenugui towels. Best traditional souvenir shopping in Tokyo.

Jun 25
Fushimi Inari at Dawn + Nishiki Market + Yasaka Shrine + Gion
5:30 AM. Non-negotiable. The most important early morning of the Japan leg.
5:30 AM⭐ Fushimi Inari Taisha — Dawn
Set multiple alarms. Thousands of vermilion torii gates winding 4km up a mountain — at dawn in complete silence with morning mist drifting through the gates, this is otherworldly. By 9 AM it's tour groups and selfie sticks. At 5:30 AM it belongs to you. Walk as far up as you have energy for — the lower gates are the iconic composition but the higher you go the more dramatic and solitary it becomes. Use the tripod for long exposure shots of the gated path receding into the mist. Plan 2-3 hours here minimum.
Photo
8:00 AMVermillion Espresso — Post-Inari Coffee
Right at the base of Fushimi Inari. Small specialty coffee shop. Get a flat white or pour-over and decompress after the climb. The name is a nod to the red gates. Well deserved after a 5:30 AM start.
Coffee
9:30 AMReturn to hotel — rest, freshen up
Short train back to central Kyoto. Shower, change, eat something at the hotel or nearby konbini. Recover before the afternoon push.
11:00 AMNishiki Market — Kyoto's Kitchen
400-year-old covered market street, 390m long, 100+ stalls. Kyoto's food culture in one alley. Must try: yudofu (hot silken tofu, a Kyoto specialty), tsukemono (pickled vegetables in dozens of varieties), tamagoyaki on a stick, matcha mochi, fresh yuba (tofu skin). Graze the full street — budget ¥2,000-3,000. Also excellent for food souvenirs: pickled goods, matcha products, dried goods packaged for travel.
FoodSouvenirs
2:00 PMGion afternoon walk + Tatsumi Bridge
Walk Hanamikoji Street in the afternoon light — different from the evening, quieter, better for photography. Turn onto the smaller lanes east of Hanamikoji — Shirakawa canal runs through here, lined with weeping willows. Tatsumi Bridge is a small arched bridge over the canal — the view with the canal, lanterns, and machiya is one of the quintessential Kyoto photos. Look for geiko or maiko in the late afternoon — rare but possible during this hour as they go to appointments.
Photo
4:00 PMYasaka Shrine
At the eastern end of Shijo Street, right at the entrance to Gion — you're already on this street. Founded in 656 AD. The main gate (Nishiromon) is the large red gate at the end of Shijo — iconic shot looking down the street toward it. The shrine complex has lanterns that light up at dusk.
Photo
7:00 PMDinner — Gionshinbashi Nakatani
Traditional kaiseki restaurant hidden in a narrow Gion alley. Seasonal seafood, wooden beams, tatami. Reservations recommended. A proper Kyoto dinner experience.

6:30 AM⭐ Arashiyama Bamboo Grove at Sunrise
Early or forget it — same rule as Fushimi Inari. The grove path is narrow, bamboo towers 20m overhead blocking the sky, morning light filters through in green-gold shafts. At sunrise this is breathtaking. By 9 AM it's shoulder to shoulder. Go straight to the grove from the train. Spend 45-60 min here, walk the full path, shoot from multiple positions. Tripod for low-light morning shots.
Photo

Right next to the Bamboo Grove. The Sogenchi Garden — a Zen garden with central pond, raked gravel, and the Arashiyama mountains as the borrowed landscape backdrop. Sit on the veranda of the main hall and look out — the composition of pond, moss, stone, and mountains is perfectly balanced. Garden entrance ¥500.
